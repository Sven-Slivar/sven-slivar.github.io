What I learned

  1. 1
    Telegraph the input, not the plan.

    Cues bound to the exact button (attack side, projectile colour, the glowing ring) were read almost perfectly. A preview of the whole pattern was correct information that nobody had attention to spare for mid-fight.

  2. 2
    Make failure legible and people stay.

    Separate labels for WRONG DIR!, WRONG TYPE! and MISS! told players exactly what to fix. With a 7.5% win rate, people still logged 255 runs, and some played 30+.

  3. 3
    Keep the language fixed and scale the timing.

    Every tier uses the same cues; difficulty comes only from spawn delay and the perfect window (0.40 → 0.07 s). Players called the phase escalation “harder in a good way” (76%, no negative answers).

  4. 4
    What's obvious to the designer isn't obvious to the player.

    The sequence bar and the ring's timing edge made sense to me and confused testers. Measuring with logs plus surveys showed the gap that playtesting by feel didn't.
